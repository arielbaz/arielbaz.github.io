About

A soldering iron, a terminal, too much curiosity.

Ariel. Jerusalem, with one foot in France. A workshop, a terminal, and a habit of wanting to understand how everything works.

I make a living repairing, building and explaining technical things. No consulting desk, no startup: a workshop.

Machines to get running again, sites to deliver, invitations to design, servers to keep up. The common thread is always the same: understand a system, find what’s wrong, and put it back in shape — cleanly.

What I do, concretely

ARIBAZ Electronics. Component-level diagnosis, micro-soldering, repair of motherboards, controllers and e-bike batteries. When the manufacturer’s quote says “unrepairable”, that’s often where it gets interesting.

ARIBAZ Web & Systems. Sites, shops, automation. Proxmox, NAS, tested backups, 10 GbE network. The kind of infrastructure that makes no noise because it never goes down.

ARIBAZ Events. Digital invitations, wedding cards, sound. Projects where the detail shows immediately — and so must be right.

How I work

Diagnosis before the quote. I don’t replace a part to see what happens. We measure, we test, then we decide.

The announced price is the price paid. If something unexpected changes things, I warn you before going on — never after.

I document what I do. A system you don’t understand is a system you depend on. That’s not the goal.

I say when I don’t know. And when it can’t be repaired, I say that too. It’s more useful than billing hope.

The best hardware is the one you haven’t thrown away yet. And the best software is the one you can read.

What I believe, technically

Open source, self-hosting, encryption, data sovereignty. Not out of ideology: because they are the only options that leave me the owner of my tools. A cloud subscription is a rental. A backup you can’t restore is a decoration.

That’s also why I repair. A repaired machine is one more machine that doesn’t end up in a landfill, and one more user who doesn’t depend on a replacement every two years.

The rest

Human psychology, communication, education, how commerce works. Understanding how people learn and decide helps me as much to repair a computer as to explain a tip on WhatsApp. It’s the same trade: making the complex obvious.

Why this site

I dig through the internet. Sometimes I find things. That’s the whole method: no paid watch, no pre-digested list — curiosity, tests, and a strict sort.

News goes out first on a WhatsApp channel, because that’s where people are. But a chat thread is not a library: this site files everything, fixes the typos, and keeps the good tips findable two years later.

Two to four posts a month. No cookie, no tracker, no advertising. That too is a design choice.
