Bitchat: the messenger that keeps working when the network dies

No internet, no account, no phone number. Messages hop from phone to phone over Bluetooth, and nobody can cut the conversation.

Packed stadium, internet outage, a cut during a protest, a plane or a trip off-grid: in those moments, WhatsApp and Signal go silent.

Bitchat is the messenger that keeps working when everything else is down. It sends your messages straight from phone to phone over Bluetooth. No internet, no data plan, no account, no central server to switch off.

What you get

  • Chat without internet: every phone relays messages to nearby devices, up to 7 hops away.
  • No account, no number: pick a nickname, that's it.
  • End-to-end encrypted: private messages use the Noise protocol — even the phones relaying them can't read them.
  • A public channel and private messages: #mesh is open to every device in range, DMs stay between you and the recipient.
  • A panic mode: three taps on the logo wipe everything, instantly.

Why everyone is talking about it

The idea is Doris Lima's, development is led by Jack Dorsey (Twitter's co-founder) with Block, and everything is open source. Bitchat went viral during internet shutdowns: protests in Madagascar and India, disaster zones, saturated networks.

India even ordered GitHub to block the project's repository — the best proof the tool works.

Online, it complements the mesh with the Nostr protocol: when Bluetooth isn't enough, messages keep flowing through Nostr relays, and geolocated channels let you talk to people in your neighbourhood.

The ARIBAZ pro-tip — three limits to know

The mesh only exists if others have the app. Each Bluetooth hop covers about ten metres: without humans relaying around you, Bitchat only talks to your immediate surroundings. It's a crowd messenger, not a living-room one.

On iPhone, keep the app open. iOS throttles Bluetooth in the background: the app has to stay on screen to send and receive. On Android it's far more flexible.

Be honest with yourself: the app is young. Launched in July 2025, it hasn't had a full independent security audit yet, and its creators say so. For sensitive everyday conversations, Signal remains the reference. Bitchat is the insurance policy for the day the network falls.

Getting started

  1. Install Bitchat Mesh from the App Store (iPhone, macOS) or Play Store (Android). On Android you can also grab the official APK on GitHub — beware of lookalikes.
  2. Turn on Bluetooth, open the app, pick a nickname.
  3. Join #mesh to talk to every device in range, or send a private message to someone nearby.
  4. Do a real test with someone next to you, before you actually need it.

Verdict

It's a digital first-aid kit: you install it before you need it, because the day the internet goes down, it's too late to download. Two minutes today, one possible conversation tomorrow.
